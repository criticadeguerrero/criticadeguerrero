# Operations and ownership

## Purpose

This document records the continuity model for Crítica de Guerrero. It deliberately records roles and procedures, not passwords, recovery codes, payment details, or other secrets.

## Account roles

| Account or person | Intended role | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `criticadeguerrero@gmail.com` | Legacy continuity and institutional ownership account | Older publication address. Keep as a recovery-capable owner for registrar, archival services, analytics, storage, and core publishing services where appropriate. |
| `criticaguerrero@gmail.com` | Current operational account | Newer address for day-to-day administration, repository access, Netlify, editorial tooling, and shared operational workflows where appropriate. |
| Uncle's personal account | Editor and approved recovery contact | Do not make this the sole owner of the domain, hosting, billing, or recovery path. Give only the access needed for editorial work. |

## Domain model

- `criticadeguerrero.com.mx` is a long-term publication asset and should be registered in a publication-controlled account.
- The current production domain configuration must be documented in the password manager and reviewed before any DNS change.
- Enable auto-renewal, two-factor authentication, registrar lock, and at least two independent recovery contacts.
- Keep registrar invoices, renewal dates, account owner details, and support contacts in a private operational record.

## Access rules

- Use separate named accounts whenever a provider supports them.
- Give editors only the minimum role needed to create, edit, upload, preview, and publish content.
- Keep administrator, registrar, DNS, billing, and deployment permissions limited to designated operators.
- Review access after staffing or family-role changes.
- Store credentials and recovery codes in a shared password manager or an offline family-controlled record, never in GitHub, website files, CMS content, issues, pull requests, or chat exports.

## Recovery procedure

1. Confirm who owns the service and which institutional account is the recovery owner.
2. Use the password manager or private recovery record; do not post secrets in a repository or email thread.
3. If a domain or hosting provider cannot verify ownership, gather invoices, historic notices, account emails, and proof of prior publication use.
4. Record the incident, resolution, account changes, and next renewal date in the private operations record.

## Review cadence

Review domain renewal, recovery contacts, 2FA, account ownership, and backup/export status at least twice per year.
